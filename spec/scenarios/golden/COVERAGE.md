Coverage is measured per block against that block's own axis product. Legal cells are

when a charge model, aggregation or interval is added — a new capability shows up here as
uncovered instead of silently making these percentages a lie.

`RISK` is fixes × the fraction of the block still uncovered — expected unguarded bug
density. It ranks what to write next by evidence rather than by taste, and decays to 0
as a block approaches full coverage.

### B17 — Feature stacks

B15 proves each pair in isolation; a real invoice carries several reducers at once. From three up the question stops being "which of the two runs first" and becomes whether a stage is still handed the right base after everything before it has already moved it — which no pair row can observe. Cells are the derived subsets of the reducer list, so the block grows by itself when the pipeline gains a stage.

- Axes: `stack` (16) × `observed_via` (3)
- Legal cells: 48 · covered: 48 (100%)

### B18 — Mid-flight changes

Every other block sets a configuration up and then bills it. Real customers change the configuration while an invoice is in flight — a coupon replaced between two threshold invoices, a tax rate edited before the period closes, a charge amount corrected on a draft. What matters is which invoices re-read the change and which were already fixed: a draft re-reads, a finalized invoice must not, and a preview should agree with whichever it is predicting.

- Axes: `mutation` (6) × `stage` (4) × `observed_via` (4)
- Legal cells: 84 · covered: 84 (100%)

### B19 — Multiplicity

B17 asks what happens when different reducers meet on one invoice. This asks what happens when the SAME one appears twice, which is a different question and an unasserted one: two instances either simply add, or they compete for the same money — and if they compete, something decides which goes first and therefore which survives to the next period. Every one of those orderings exists in the code and none is covered. Applied coupons are sorted metric-limited, then plan-limited, then oldest first, and skipped entirely once the sub_total reaches zero (credits/applied_coupons_service.rb:50-53). Wallets go by ascending priority. Credit notes and prepaid credits each draw on a balance that the one before it has already reduced.

- Axes: `feature` (8) × `arrangement` (3) × `observed_via` (3)
- Legal cells: 53 · covered: 52 (98%)
- Uncovered (first 1 of 1): `charge/unequal/preview`

### B20 — Identity churn

The bugs that reach production are not "this feature is wrong" — they are "this feature could not find its own state after something was reconfigured". Lago recovers carried state by joining on record ids: progressive-billing credits match fees on charge_id, charge_filter_id and grouped_by; an already-paid pay-in-advance fee is matched on charge_id and pay_in_advance_event_id; a limited coupon on plan_id and billable_metric_id. A plan override, a subscription override or an upgrade rewrites those ids, and the lookup then returns nothing rather than failing — so the credit is lost, or the fee is deleted, and the invoice is quietly wrong. Cells are the intersection of what a churn rewrites and what a state is found by, so every one of them names a join actually at risk.

- Axes: `churn` (6) × `carried_state` (6) × `observed_via` (4)
- Legal cells: 64 · covered: 64 (100%)

### B21 — Surface parity

Sixteen of twenty blocks had never asserted anything but a finalized invoice, and BIL-537 is what that costs: B13 covers a fixed-charge units change thirty-six ways and cannot see the bug, because not one of its rows holds an invoice open long enough to refresh it. The fix is not a fourth axis on every block — that would triple more than a thousand cells, most of them cases where draft and final provably agree. It is one cell per (block, surface), asserting for a representative scenario from each block what that surface makes of it. Subject blocks are the ones whose behaviour can actually differ: they involve a reducer gated on finalization (calculate_fees_service.rb:364-379) or are recomputed on refresh.

- Axes: `subject_block` (8) × `surface` (3)
- Legal cells: 24 · covered: 24 (100%)
